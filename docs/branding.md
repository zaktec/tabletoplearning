# TableTopLearning Brand Guidelines

## Document purpose

This document defines how TableTopLearning should look, sound and feel across the website, web application, social media, email and marketing materials.

Use it when creating or reviewing public-facing design, copy, navigation, forms, campaigns, presentations or brand assets. It does not define technical architecture or product functionality; those belong in [technical-reference.md](technical-reference.md) and [project-overview.md](project-overview.md).

## 1. Brand name and spelling

The brand name is:

**TableTopLearning**

Always use the exact capitalisation and spacing shown above.

Correct:

- TableTopLearning
- TableTopLearning courses
- Contact TableTopLearning

Incorrect:

- Tabletoplearning
- TabletopLearning
- Table Top Learning
- TableTop Learning
- TTL Learning

`TTL` may be used inside the approved icon or as a short internal reference. It must not replace **TableTopLearning** in normal public copy.

## 2. Brand purpose and positioning

### Purpose

TableTopLearning helps learners make steady progress through structured online courses and clear tutor support.

### Positioning

TableTopLearning is a warm, practical and trustworthy learning provider for people who want:

- A clear starting point.
- Lessons arranged in a manageable order.
- Visible progression through a subject.
- A choice of independent or tutor-supported learning.
- Straightforward information for learners and families.

The brand is not positioned as a high-pressure examination factory, an entertainment product or a fully automated learning system. It should feel human, organised and dependable.

### Brand promise

**Clear learning pathways with support that fits the learner.**

This is a positioning statement, not a guarantee of a specific academic result.

## 3. Target audience

### Primary audiences

- Learners exploring Maths, Computer Science, AI, English and related subjects.
- GCSE, A-level and Functional Skills learners.
- Beginners developing coding and technology skills.
- Parents and carers comparing structured learning support.
- People returning to education who want a clear pathway.

### Audience needs

The audience should quickly understand:

- What subjects are available.
- Which courses are coming soon.
- Where to start.
- How learning is structured.
- What tutor support is shown.
- What the next action will do.

Some visitors may feel uncertain about online learning or examination preparation. Communication should reduce confusion without being patronising.

## 4. Brand personality

TableTopLearning is:

- **Friendly:** welcoming and human.
- **Encouraging:** focused on manageable progress.
- **Professional:** accurate, calm and well organised.
- **Practical:** clear about what the learner can do next.
- **Trustworthy:** honest about availability and limitations.
- **Modern:** comfortable with technology without chasing trends.
- **Dependable:** consistent in language, layout and expectations.

TableTopLearning is not:

- Childish or overly playful.
- Aggressive or sales-heavy.
- Formal, distant or bureaucratic.
- Exaggerated or full of hype.
- Vague about what is available.
- Visually noisy or trend-led.

## 5. Tone of voice

### Core voice

Write in plain English with a calm, direct and encouraging tone.

Good TableTopLearning copy:

- Starts with the visitor’s goal or next step.
- Uses short sentences and familiar words.
- Explains structure and support clearly.
- Distinguishes current features from future plans.
- Sounds professional without sounding corporate.

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | Confident and welcoming | “Structured learning with real tutor support.” |
| Course information | Clear and practical | “Build core skills and prepare for Maths examinations.” |
| Calls to action | Direct and specific | “Explore Maths” |
| Coming-soon content | Honest and helpful | “Coming soon” |
| Contact guidance | Warm and efficient | “Tell us which course or support plan you are asking about.” |
| Safeguarding or privacy | Calm, precise and serious | “Include enough detail for the team to respond to your request.” |
| Errors or unavailable features | Reassuring and factual | “Learner accounts are not available yet.” |
| Social media | Concise and useful | “Starting Python? Begin with variables, input and simple calculations.” |

Avoid changing personality between channels. Social content may be shorter and more conversational, but it should still be accurate and calm.

## 6. Key messages

Use these messages as a hierarchy, not as a requirement to place every phrase on every page.

### Primary message

**Structured online courses with real tutor support.**

### Supporting messages

- Learn week by week.
- Follow a clear learning pathway.
- Build skills in manageable stages.
- Choose the support level that suits you.
- Start at the right level and progress when ready.
- Clear lessons, practical activities and tutor guidance.

### Trust messages

- Course availability is shown clearly.
- Support varies by course and plan.
- Current terms are confirmed before a learner starts.
- Questions can be discussed before choosing a plan.

### Message boundaries

Do not promise:

- Guaranteed grades.
- Guaranteed confidence or employment.
- Instant mastery.
- Admission to a school, university or job.
- Results that have not been evidenced.
- Services that the website or organisation has not confirmed.

## 7. Logo and wordmark

### Current logo system

The website uses a compact `TTL` icon beside the full **TableTopLearning** wordmark.

The full lockup is preferred for:

- Website headers and footers.
- Marketing pages.
- Email headers.
- Presentations.
- Documents with enough horizontal space.

The `TTL` icon may be used alone for:

- Favicons.
- Social profile images.
- Small square interface spaces.
- Approved app icons.

When the icon appears alone, the surrounding profile name, page title or nearby text should identify TableTopLearning.

### Usage rules

- Keep the icon and wordmark in their approved proportions.
- Keep the wordmark on one line when space allows.
- Use the icon to the left of the wordmark in horizontal layouts.
- Keep clear space around the lockup at least equal to half the icon width.
- Keep the website icon at least 36px square.
- Use the full wordmark whenever clarity is more important than space.
- Place the logo on simple white, cream or approved dark backgrounds.
- Use an approved exported asset for external marketing once one exists.

### Do not

- Change the spelling or capitalisation.
- Stretch, squash, rotate or skew the logo.
- Separate the wordmark into multiple words.
- Add shadows, outlines or decorative effects.
- Recolour it with unrelated accent colours.
- Place it over a busy photograph without a clear background area.
- recreate the logo differently for each channel.
- Treat a website screenshot as a production logo asset.

The current website lockup is rendered with HTML and CSS. Before high-resolution print or large-format use, create and approve a proper vector asset that follows these rules.

## 8. Colour palette

### Primary colours

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | TableTop orange | `#bd5500` | Primary buttons, small accents and important active states |
| Primary hover | Deep orange | `#963f00` | Button hover, strong text links and selected emphasis |
| Canvas | Warm cream | `#fff8ec` | Main page and marketing background |
| Surface | White | `#ffffff` | Header, footer, cards, forms and content panels |

### Secondary and supporting colours

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soft accent | Pale orange | `#fff3df` | Secondary buttons, icon backgrounds, badges and numbered markers |
| Heading | Warm charcoal | `#241a14` | Headings and high-emphasis copy |
| Body text | Brown-grey | `#594a40` | Main body copy |
| Muted text | Soft brown-grey | `#78695e` | Supporting and explanatory copy |
| Border | Warm neutral | `#e8d9c7` | Card, field and section borders |
| Focus | Dark amber | `#7a3d00` | Focus indicators and accessible interaction emphasis |

### Colour usage rules

- Use warm cream for the main canvas.
- Use white for primary content surfaces.
- Use orange for important actions and small accents.
- Use pale orange sparingly for supportive emphasis.
- Use warm charcoal for headings.
- Use neutral borders by default.
- Reserve orange borders for hover, focus, selected or recommended states.
- Keep large areas calm; orange should guide attention rather than dominate the page.
- Do not create subject-specific rainbow colours unless a future brand update formally defines them.
- Never rely on colour alone to communicate availability, errors or selection.

### CSS source of truth

```css
  --color-primary: #bd5500;
  --color-primary-hover: #963f00;
  --color-background: #fff8ec;
  --color-surface: #ffffff;
  --color-surface-soft: #fff3df;

  --color-text-muted: #78695e;
  --color-border: #e8d9c7;
  --color-focus: #7a3d00;
}
```

Use these tokens on the website instead of introducing near-duplicate colour values.

## 9. Typography

### Font families

Preferred heading stack:

```css
"Poppins", "Inter", "Segoe UI", sans-serif
```

Preferred body stack:

```css
"Inter", "Segoe UI", Roboto, Arial, sans-serif
```

Use the same hierarchy in marketing tools where possible. When Poppins or Inter is unavailable, use a clean, widely available sans-serif rather than a decorative substitute.

### Type hierarchy

- Headings should be compact, moderately bold and easy to scan.
- Body text should generally be 15–16px with a line height between 1.45 and 1.65.
- Supporting card and pricing text should be at least 14px.
- Uppercase eyebrow labels may be 10–12px with increased letter spacing.
- Buttons should use concise, bold labels.
- Keep paragraphs at a comfortable reading width.
- Avoid all-uppercase paragraphs or long headings.

### Emphasis

- Use headings and layout to create hierarchy before using bold.
- Use bold for short, important details such as an email address or plan name.
- Avoid long italic passages.
- Do not underline text unless it is a link.

## 10. Buttons, forms and interface style

### Primary buttons

Use for the main action in a section.

- Solid TableTop orange background.
- White text.
- Rounded pill shape.
- Minimum 44px touch target.
- Deep-orange hover state.
- Visible keyboard focus.

Examples:

- Explore courses
- Browse courses
- Prepare email

### Secondary buttons

Use for useful but lower-priority actions.

- Pale-orange or neutral background.
- Deep-orange text.
- Same sizing and rounded style as primary buttons.

Examples:

- View learning pathways
- Explore Maths

### Button writing rules

- Begin with a clear verb.
- Describe what happens next.
- Keep labels to two or three words when practical.
- Use sentence case.
- Do not use vague labels such as “Click here”.
- Do not label an action “Send” if it only prepares an email.

### Cards and panels

- Use white surfaces against the cream canvas.
- Use consistent rounded corners.
- Keep borders neutral by default.
- Use subtle hover movement only on interactive cards.
- Keep shadows subtle and uncommon.
- Avoid turning every piece of content into a separate card.

### Forms

- Place a visible label above every field.
- Explain what submission will do.
- Mark required fields through native behavior and clear wording.
- Keep fields at least 44px high.
- Use neutral borders with visible hover and focus states.
- Use helpful field groupings and a single clear primary action.
- Avoid collecting information that is not needed.
- Provide clear success and error feedback if direct form processing is introduced.

### Navigation

- Keep the full brand lockup on the left.
- Use short, familiar navigation labels.
- Keep the most important action visually distinct.
- Preserve keyboard operation and a clear mobile menu.

## 11. Image, illustration and icon style

### Photography

Photography should feel:

- Natural and believable.
- Inclusive across age, background and learning needs.
- Focused on real study, problem-solving or tutor interaction.
- Warmly lit and calm.
- Relevant to the accompanying message.

Avoid:

- Generic corporate handshakes.
- Staged celebrations or exaggerated success.
- Busy classroom scenes with no clear focus.
- Images that imply facilities, staff or outcomes that cannot be verified.
- Heavy filters or unrelated colour treatments.

### Illustration

Use simple, modern illustrations with:

- Clear educational relevance.
- Limited brand-colour accents.
- Plenty of negative space.
- A professional rather than childish style.

Do not mix several unrelated illustration styles in one campaign.

### Icons

- Prefer simple outline icons.
- Use consistent stroke weight and rounded line endings.
- Use the approved orange and neutral palette.
- Place icons on pale-orange backgrounds when a container is needed.
- Keep icons supportive rather than dominant.
- Use subject-specific symbols only when their meaning is clear.
- Hide decorative website icons from screen readers.
- Pair unfamiliar icons with text.

Do not use a mix of emoji, filled clip art and outline icons in the same interface.

## 12. Writing rules

### General rules

- Prefer **learner** over **student**, except in an authentic quotation.
- Address the reader directly with “you” where helpful.
- Use active voice.
- Keep sentences concise.
- Explain one idea at a time.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Use British English spelling.
- Use ampersands only in established labels such as “AI & Machine Learning”.
- Write prices clearly, for example `£49/month` in compact UI or `£49 per month` in prose.
- Explain abbreviations the first time if the audience may not know them.
- Use “A-level” in prose unless an approved qualification title requires different styling.
- Write “email”, “online” and “website” without hyphens.

### Accuracy rules

- Distinguish current, coming-soon and future features.
- State plan features exactly as approved.
- Do not imply that displayed pricing creates a contractual offer.
- Do not describe the login page as functional.
- Do not say the contact form sends data directly; it prepares an email.
- Do not invent tutors, testimonials, statistics, accreditations or outcomes.
- Preserve authentic quotations exactly, apart from approved accessibility corrections.

### Reading level

Aim for language that a general parent or secondary-age learner can understand on the first reading. Replace education or technology jargon with a short explanation.

## 13. Preferred and avoided language

### Words and phrases to use

- Learner
- Structured learning
- Clear learning pathway
- Step by step
- Week by week
- Tutor support
- Tutor guidance
- Practical activities
- Build skills
- Make steady progress
- Choose the right level
- Continue when ready
- Coming soon
- Explore courses
- Ask about a course

### Words and phrases to avoid

- Student, unless required by an authentic quotation or formal title
- Guaranteed results
- Guaranteed grades
- Transform your life
- Revolutionary
- World-class
- Best-in-class
- Effortless
- Master instantly
- Pass every exam
- Fail-proof
- One-size-fits-all
- Cheap
- Buy now
- Limited-time pressure language without a genuine, approved deadline
- Fully personalised, unless a real service and process support the claim
- AI-powered, unless the specific feature exists and is explained

### Better alternatives

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| “Master Maths instantly” | “Build Maths skills step by step” |
| “Guaranteed exam success” | “Prepare with structured lessons and practice” |
| “Buy now” | “Explore courses” |
| “The perfect course for everyone” | “Choose the course that fits your current level” |
| “Unlimited tutor access” | State the actual tutor contact included |
| “Submit form” | “Prepare email” or a specific approved action |

## 15. Channel guidance

### Website and web application

- Prioritise clarity, navigation and accessible interaction.
- Keep main actions visible.
- Use existing components and design tokens.
- Label availability and limitations honestly.

### Social media

- Lead with one useful idea.
- Use concise captions and one clear action.
- Keep graphics simple and recognisably on-brand.
- Avoid unverified learner stories, results or urgency.

### Marketing materials

- Use the full logo lockup where space allows.
- Use the warm cream, white and orange palette.
- Keep claims specific and supportable.
- Give course, audience and next-step information enough space.

### Email

- Use a clear subject line.
- Address the recipient politely.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- State the action or next step clearly.
- Avoid placing sensitive learner details in broad mailing lists.

## 16. Accessibility requirements

Brand consistency includes accessibility.

- Meet WCAG AA colour contrast for normal text and controls.
- Do not rely on colour alone.
- Keep body text readable and scalable.
- Use semantic headings in a logical order.
- Use descriptive link and button labels.
- Provide text alternatives for meaningful images.
- Hide decorative images and icons from assistive technology.
- Keep keyboard focus visible.
- Maintain comfortable touch targets.
- Caption meaningful video and provide transcripts when appropriate.
- Avoid flashing, rapid motion and unnecessary animation.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep copy understandable without specialist knowledge.

Accessibility must not be removed to achieve a preferred visual treatment.

## 17. Consistency checklist

Before publishing any public-facing material, confirm:

- The name is written as **TableTopLearning**.
- The purpose and audience are clear.
- The tone is warm, professional and practical.
- Claims are accurate and supportable.
- Current and coming-soon features are distinguished.
- The approved logo arrangement is used correctly.
- Colours come from the approved palette.
- Typography is readable and consistent.
- Buttons describe the actual next action.
- Images and icons share a coherent style.
- **Learner** is used instead of **student** where appropriate.
- Mobile and keyboard use have been considered.
- Colour contrast and alternative text are sufficient.
- The material looks and sounds like the website.

If a new channel or asset cannot follow these rules cleanly, pause and obtain a brand decision rather than creating an inconsistent exception.
